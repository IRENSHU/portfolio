Amsterdam in 3 days

An itinerary you can actually walk: travel time between stops is built in, timed tickets are flagged, and the budget is spelled out — no “see everything in a weekend”.

  • 3 days, 2–3 nights
  • from €340 per person
  • On foot and by tram
  • Best in April–May, September

Before you book

Four things worth knowing before you buy the tickets.

📅

When to go

April and May bring tulips and long daylight, September is warm and far less crowded. Winter is cheap, but it gets dark at 4:30 pm and the wind off the water is relentless.

🎫

Book tickets ahead

The Rijksmuseum, the Van Gogh Museum and the Anne Frank House admit visitors by timed slot only. Anne Frank slots open six weeks ahead and are gone within a day.

🚋

Getting around

You can cross the old centre on foot in an hour. For everything else there are GVB trams and metro, about €9 for a day ticket; the ferries across the IJ are free.

🧾

City tax

Amsterdam charges 12.5% of the room rate. You pay it at check-in, and booking sites usually leave it out of the headline price — the calculator below includes it.

Day by day

Times include getting from one place to the next, not teleporting between them. Switch days below — each one works on its own.

Day 1. The centre, the canals and a feel for the city

About 6 km on foot · no advance tickets needed

  • 09:30

    Dam Square and the Royal Palace

    Dam, Koninklijk Paleis

    The heart of the city and a handy point to measure every other distance from. Go inside if you like interiors: 17th-century halls and an audio guide at the door. Outside are the New Church and the national war memorial.

    • 1.5 hours
    • €12.50
    • Fine from outside too
  • 10 minutes on foot along Kalverstraat
  • 11:30

    The Begijnhof courtyard

    Begijnhof

    A silent 14th-century courtyard two minutes from the crowds: a lawn, a wooden house from 1528 and complete quiet — people still live here, so keep your voice down.

    • 40 minutes
    • Free
    • Closes at 6 pm
  • 8 minutes west, towards the canals
  • 12:30

    Lunch in the Nine Streets

    De 9 Straatjes

    Nine short streets stitched between the canals: small cafés, cheese shops, vintage stores. A good place to try the local classics — herring in a bun, or apple pie with cinnamon.

    • 1 hour
    • Lunch €15–25
  • 5 minutes to the jetty on Prinsengracht
  • 14:30

    A canal cruise

    Grachtengordel

    The 17th-century canal ring is a UNESCO site, and it reads completely differently from the water. Boats leave every 20–30 minutes; the evening cruise costs more but the bridges are lit.

    • 1 hour
    • €18–25
    • On weekends book in the morning
  • 12 minutes on foot along the Singel
  • 16:30

    The floating flower market

    Bloemenmarkt

    The only flower market in the world that sits on the water. Tulip bulbs come with an export certificate — without it they will not clear customs, so ask for the paperwork on the spot.

    • 30 minutes
    • Closes at 5:30 pm
  • 10 minutes on foot
  • 18:00

    Evening on Leidseplein

    Leidseplein

    A square of terraces, street musicians and theatres. After that it is either dinner here or a quiet walk back along the water — the lit canals are the best view of the day.

    • Dinner €25–40

Day 2. The Museum Quarter

About 4 km on foot · two timed tickets, buy in advance

  • 09:00

    Rijksmuseum

    Rijksmuseum

    It opens at nine, and for the first hour the galleries are nearly empty — the best time to stand in front of The Night Watch. Seeing all of it is impossible: pick two or three rooms you genuinely care about and let the rest go.

    • 3 hours
    • €25
    • Timed entry
  • 6 minutes across Museumplein
  • 12:30

    Lunch on Museum Square

    Museumplein

    In good weather the easiest option is takeaway food on the grass — the square was made for it. The famous pond next to it becomes an ice rink in winter.

    • 1 hour
    • €12–20
  • 4 minutes on foot
  • 13:30

    Van Gogh Museum

    Van Gogh Museum

    The largest collection of his work, hung chronologically — from dark peasant scenes to the last wheat fields. Tickets are online only and tied to an entry time; there is no ticket desk.

    • 2 hours
    • €24
    • Timed entry
  • 8 minutes on foot
  • 16:00

    Vondelpark

    Vondelpark

    The city's main park: ponds, cyclists, an open-air stage in summer. After two museums this hour matters as much as the museums did.

    • 1 hour
    • Free
  • Tram 2, 12 minutes — or 25 minutes on foot
  • 17:30

    The Jordaan

    Jordaan

    A former working-class district turned into the most liveable one: narrow streets, hidden courtyards, small galleries and cafés full of locals rather than tourists. Good for dinner.

    • Evening
    • Dinner €25–40

Day 3. Anne Frank, the water and a look outside the city

About 3 km on foot · one ticket you must book well ahead

  • 09:00

    Anne Frank House

    Anne Frank Huis

    The house where the family hid for two years, and the bookcase that covered the entrance. It is quiet and cramped inside; afterwards it helps to sit by the canal rather than rush on.

    • 1.5 hours
    • €16
    • Slots open six weeks ahead
  • 2 minutes on foot
  • 11:00

    Westerkerk

    Westerkerk

    Anne Frank could hear these bells from the annex. The tower is open by guided tour every half hour — the stairs are steep, but the whole canal ring lies below you.

    • 45 minutes
    • Tower €10
  • Tram 13, 10 minutes to Central Station
  • 12:30

    The ferry to the north bank

    Amsterdam Noord, NDSM

    Ferries leave the back of Central Station every few minutes and cost nothing — bicycles and people board together. On the far side is the old NDSM shipyard: murals, warehouses, a flea market at weekends. The A'DAM Lookout tower next door has a swing over the edge of the roof.

    • 2–3 hours
    • Ferry free
    • Lookout €16.50
  • Ferry back, 15 minutes
  • 16:00

    The public library

    OBA Oosterdok

    Seven floors, a lift to the top and an open terrace over the harbour and the old town — free, and almost tourist-free. A good place to say goodbye to the city.

    • 1 hour
    • Free

If you would rather leave town: swap the north bank for Zaanse Schans — windmills, a cheese farm and green wooden houses, 17 minutes by train from Central Station. Half a day, about €8 each way.

Common questions

Are three days enough?

For the city itself, yes — the centre is small. Not for the museums: there are more than seventy, and in three days it is sane to see two or three rather than race down a list.

Should I rent a bike?

It looks romantic, but not on day one: traffic is dense, cyclists have their own etiquette and the fines are real. If you want to, rent on day three and ride in the park.

Is the city card worth it?

The I amsterdam City Card pays off if you do three or more museums a day and use transport heavily. At the calmer pace of this itinerary, separate tickets work out cheaper.

What about the weather?

Rain can arrive any day and usually passes quickly, but the wind off the water turns umbrellas inside out — a hooded jacket is more useful. Wear shoes you can do six kilometres in.

How this template works

The page is built so the next article takes an evening to put together without touching the code: you replace the text and the photos, everything else keeps working.

  • Day tabs switch without reloading, and when the page is printed all three days come out one after another — the article can be taken along on paper.
  • The budget calculator keeps its prices and the city-tax rate at the top of app.js, one number per line. For another city you change those numbers.
  • The checklist is stored in the reader's own browser, nothing is sent anywhere, and no consent banner is needed for it.
  • The map loads only on click. Until then there are no requests to outside servers: faster, and no awkward questions about tracking.
  • Two language versions live at separate addresses, index.html and en.html, linked with hreflang so search engines serve the right one.
  • Structured data — Article and breadcrumbs in JSON-LD, and every image has its own alt in the right language. Photo slots are labelled with the size and a sample alt.
